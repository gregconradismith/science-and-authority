<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Science &amp; Authority - APSC 453 - Fall 2026</h2>


<h4 class="wp-block-heading">ISC 3280 - Tuesday and Thursday - 2:00-3:20 am<br>Greg Conradi Smith - Applied Science, Neuroscience &amp; CAMS Biomath</h4>


<p>Modern science stands as a powerful cultural authority, influencing our world for better and for worse. But what legitimizes science as an authority? Are its foundational assumptions truly self-evident? How should we, as scientists, confront the rising tide of science denial? While we recognize and value the immense benefits that science has brought to society, how do we resist the dehumanizing aspects of an increasingly technological world? And in our fast-paced, pluralistic, post-fact society, what is the status of traditional scientific values like honesty, communalism, and disinterested research?</p>


<p>This course will tackle these challenging questions through a series of case studies, examining instances where science has gone astray—both historically, such as with tobacco, eugenics, and scientific racism, and in contemporary contexts, like propaganda, surveillance, fraud, and dead-end research. We will critically engage with postmodern critiques of science, questioning the intellectual hegemony that often frames scientific ‘objectivity’ as a tool for justifying dominant cultures and preserving the status quo.</p>


<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What to expect</h2>


<p>This is a seminar-style course with readings from magazines for concerned and thoughtful citizens as well as academic literature (e.g., philosophy of science), lots of critical analysis and discussion, lectures, and student projects and presentations.</p>


<p>The intended audience is STEM majors who are interested in exploring science &amp; society issues and the meaning of science. The course will be philosophical in nature, and required readings will be challenging, but students are not expected to have significant prior coursework in philosophy or sociology.</p>


<p>There is no textbook to be purchased. Readings will be drawn from academic books and journal articles and provided in electronic format.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Evaluation and projects</h2>


<p>A serious engagement with the lectures, readings, videos, and assigned problems is absolutely required.  This will be assessed as follows.</p>


<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Assignments  (25%)</strong></h3>


<p>Written assignments will include short quizzes on readings and/or a written response to questions about required readings to be completed before class.</p>


<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Participation (15%)</strong></h3>


<p>This is a discussion course. Attendance is required.  You are expected to listen and speak.  Email me in advance if you are going to miss class. This can happen up to 2 times in the semester without negatively affecting your participation grade.   If you are managing long-term illnesses or other circumstances that could require missing more than 2 classes, you must work with the Dean of Students Office to provide documentation.</p>


<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Midterm project (20%)</h3>


<p>The midterm project will be a critical review of a suggested reading related to the course objectives.  Book choice will occur about 1/4 of the way through the semester.  The due date will be about 1/2 way through the semester.</p>


<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Term project (40%)</strong></h3>


<p>Your term project will involve independent library research and deep intellectual engagement with some aspect of the interplay between science &amp; authority that is of interest to you.</p>


<p>The project will be evaluated through</p>


<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>a preliminary written project proposal (5% of final grade),</li>


<li>an annotated bibliography and oral progress report (10%), and</li>


<li>a final deliverable (25%).</li>
</ul>


<p>Project proposals will be due shortly after the midterm project and must be approved by the instructor. The oral progress report will occur about 3/4 of the way through the semester. The final written report should be completed before the final exam week ends.</p>


<p>Students are always evaluated with reference to course objectives, never by relative performance.</p>
