<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Different Meanings of <em>Theory</em></h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In ordinary language we can distinguish between different uses of the world <strong>theory</strong>.  Sometimes theory means <strong>speculation</strong> or an accounting of a situation.  As in:  <em>My theory is that Greg talks about Norway because his wife has relatives who live there. </em> At other times the word theory means a <strong>set of principles</strong> upon which the practice of an activity or practice is based. As in Albert Einstein's famous quote:  "<em>In theory, theory and practice are the same. In practice, they are not.</em>"</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For us, in this class on <em>Science &amp; Authority</em>, I would like us to distinguish - or attempt to discriminate between - the following two usages of the word theory:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Theory as a <strong>lens</strong> or <strong>conceptual framework</strong> through which one may view or think about the world (<a rel="noreferrer noopener" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postcolonialism" target="_blank">postcolonial critical theory</a>, <a rel="noreferrer noopener" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_psychology" target="_blank">individual psychology</a>).</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Theory as a <strong>system of related, well-corroborated scientific ideas</strong> (<a rel="noreferrer noopener" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell%27s_equations" target="_blank">Maxwell's theory of electromagnetism</a>, <a rel="noreferrer noopener" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution" target="_blank">the theory of evolution</a>).</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Some caveats:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>I do <strong>not </strong>meant to suggest that, say, critical race theory is less true, or is less important than, say, insights of biological anthropologists on race and racism.  Karl Popper himself thought that valuable insights may arise from `ways of knowing' that are not scientific in his sense of being falsifiable/testable.  </li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>The `system of scientific ideas' category is not meant to imply any particular solution (or personal commitment) to the science and non-science demarcation problem, in spite of the fact that I have chosen examples from the physical and natural sciences. </li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>These two usages of the word `theory' can be distinguished but not separated.  For example, scientific values such as objectivity and disinterestedness may become conceptual frameworks that sideline others who have more at stake.  Or looking at it the other way, one might expect that certain scientific ideas that have achieved long-standing consensus would be useful lenses through which to view contemporary problems.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->
