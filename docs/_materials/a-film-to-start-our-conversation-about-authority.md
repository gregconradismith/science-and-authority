<div class="wp-block-media-text alignwide is-stacked-on-mobile wp-embed-aspect-16-9 wp-has-aspect-ratio" style="grid-template-columns:46% auto;"><figure class="wp-block-media-text__media"><img src="../../wp-content/uploads/2022/04/81dc9hnuqol._sy445_.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-3421 size-full" /></figure><div class="wp-block-media-text__content">
<p>Yale University, 1961. Stanley Milgram (Peter Sarsgaard) designs a psychology experiment that remains relevant to this day, in which people think they’re delivering painful electric shocks to an affable stranger (Jim Gaffigan) strapped into a chair in another room. Disregarding his pleas for mercy, the majority of subjects do not stop the experiment, administering what they think are near-fatal electric shocks, simply because they’ve been told to. Milgram’s exploration of authority and conformity strikes a nerve in popular culture and the scientific community. Celebrated in some circles, he is also accused of being a deceptive, manipulative monster. His wife Sasha (Winona Ryder) anchors him through it all.</p>
