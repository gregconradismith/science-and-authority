<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Naomi Oreskes - [[]]  coauthor with Erik M. Conway of&nbsp;<strong>Merchants of Doubt</strong> - a book that documents how a handful of scientists manufactured doubt on issues ranging from tobacco smoke to climate change - wrote a subsequence book titled <strong>Why Trust Science?</strong> The publisher's note on the back cover says...</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:quote -->
<blockquote class="wp-block-quote"><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>[In Why Trust Science? Naomi Oreskes] offers a bold and compelling defense of science, revealing why the social character of scientific knowledge is its greatest strength - and the greatest reason we can trust it.  The trustworthiness of scientific claims derives from the social process by which they are rigorously vetted. This process is not perfect - nothing ever is when humans are involved - but we can learn from cases where scientists got it wrong. Oreskes shows how consensus is a crucial indicator of when a scientific matter has been settled, and when the knowledge produced is likely to be trustworthy.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></blockquote>
<!-- /wp:quote -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In this course on Science &amp; Authority, we have camped out on <strong>science gone wrong and social critiques of science</strong>, perhaps to a fault.  We have emphasized how science has gone wrong (and continues to go wrong).  We have critiqued the epistemic authority of science.  </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I would like to conclude with a focus on <strong>Trust</strong>.  Why is it that science should be trusted or ... if that is too general ...  What are some practical guidelines for why you - as STEM majors - representing the next generation of American elites - ought to trust science or ... if that is too strong ... Can we at least find some way - in the midst of the critique - to give the better angels of science the respect they are due?  In practical terms, <strong>Why, How and When Ought We Trust Science?</strong>  </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
