Science & Authority is a William & Mary COLL 400 capstone course about the cultural authority of modern science: where it comes from, how it is justified, how it can be abused, and how citizens and scientists should respond when scientific claims become politically, economically, or morally consequential.

The course treats science as both an intellectual practice and a social institution. We ask what makes scientific knowledge trustworthy, what distinguishes authority from power, how expertise can serve the public good, and how scientific language can be used to obscure responsibility.

Case studies include the scientific revolution, professional authority, falsification, Galileo, tobacco and product defense, vaccination controversies, surveillance capitalism, scientism, propaganda, and the politics of uncertainty.

The course is seminar-like. Students read challenging essays, historical cases, journalism, philosophy of science, and social criticism. Discussion, careful interpretation, and original student projects are central to the work of the course.

The central habit of mind is disciplined judgment: distinguish evidence from prestige, skepticism from denial, expertise from domination, and intellectual humility from relativism.

Students should expect to read closely, speak thoughtfully, write clearly, and take responsibility for their own developing view of science in public life.
