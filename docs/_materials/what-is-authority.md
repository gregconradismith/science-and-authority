<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Defining Terms</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Defining a term&nbsp;gives that word or phrase a particular meaning within a context (e.g., within a legal document, or for purposes of this course). This specialized meaning may or may not be the colloquial meaning of the word as used in everyday language.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Defining terms is a preliminary and tentative step used in critical thinking. Similar to a mathematical axiom, defining terms is a choice that should be judged by its helpfulness and generativity.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>To find common ground with someone who thinks differently than yourself, consider sharing the process of defining terms before you discuss or debate.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Science vs. Technology</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Let us begin the process of clarifying what we are going to mean when - in the context of this course - we say the word <strong>science</strong>. It seems to me that we should make a distinction between <strong>science</strong> and&nbsp;<strong>technology</strong>, as in this quote from <em>At the Edge of History: Speculations on the Transformation of Culture</em> by William Irwin Thompson (1971).</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:quote -->
<blockquote class="wp-block-quote"><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What the world needs now is a renewed commitment to pure and basic science, not more technologies that move in utter contempt for the ecology that supports human life. (adapted)</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></blockquote>
<!-- /wp:quote -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After distinguishing between science and technology, we might further distinguish various usages of the word technology. In everyday language technology usually refers to some aspect of science that is applied for practical purposes, to alter or control nature, often as this occurs in industry.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Examples of technologies that are important in biomedicine include: DNA sequencing, computed tomography&nbsp;(CT) scans, and mRNA vaccines. But sociologists are most interested in how technology is applied to control society or alter human nature. Medical technologies that could serve this function include `designer' psychotropic pharmaceuticals and gene editing using CRISPR/Cas9.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">What is the adjective `scientific' going to mean?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Another example of defining of terms that will be important to this course is the following. When we say something is <strong>scientific</strong>, are we referring to the <strong>domain of knowledge</strong>? Or the <strong>principles of verification</strong> of that knowledge? Or the <strong>cultural aspects of the generation</strong> of that knowledge?</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For example, is biology - the study of life - scientific because it is living organisms that are being studied? Or because production of new biological knowledge generally (or in the some special case we are considering) involves hypotheses and experiments? Or because the biological research was funded by the National Institutes of Health, performed at a university, and peer-reviewed before publication?</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The answer to these questions is part of the <strong>demarcation problem</strong> in the philosophy of science and epistemology, which is the question of <strong>how to distinguish between&nbsp;science&nbsp;and&nbsp;non-science</strong>.  We will have lots to say about this question, beginning with Karl Popper's thoughts on falsifiability of scientific theories.   </p>
