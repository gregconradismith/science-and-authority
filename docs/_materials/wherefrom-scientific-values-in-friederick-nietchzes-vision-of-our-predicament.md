<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Overview </h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The ideas of Karl Marx, Friedrich Nietzsche and Sigmund Freud are particularly relevant to understanding the modern world. Nietzsche's declaration of the "death of God" in <em>The Gay Science</em> highlights the decline of religious authority in modern Western Civilization (public religous authority if not private). As traditional sources of moral and epistemic authority lost influence, new authorities, including science, rose to fill the vacuum. This transition is critical for understanding how science has come to occupy a dominant role in legitimizing truth and authority in the modern age, but can value-neutral science substitute for universal moral law?</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Why we are watching a documentary about Friederich Nietzsche</strong></h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Why are we watching a documentary about Friedrich Nietzsche?  Several reasons.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>(1) World War II seems a good place to pause our discussion of history and philosophy of science, because of the many connections between 20th century science and technology, the space race, and the Cold War.  </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><mark style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)" class="has-inline-color has-vivid-cyan-blue-color"><code>What is the connection between Nietzsche and Word War II?</code></mark></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>(2) The ideas of Karl Marx, Sigmund Freud, and Friedrich Nietzsche are particularly relevant to understanding the modern world. To acknowledge this is not to suggest that one or more of them was completely right or completely wrong.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><mark style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)" class="has-inline-color has-vivid-cyan-blue-color">What is something that Marx got right?  What is something that Marx got wrong?  Now answer the same questions for Freud.</mark></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>(3) Nietzsche asked the question of what would replace belief in universal moral law when Western Civilization acknowledged that the Enlightenment's triumph of scientific rationality eliminated the possibility of the existence of God.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><mark style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)" class="has-inline-color has-vivid-cyan-blue-color"><code>Is scientific rationality related to the possibility of the existence of God, or the possibility of belief in God, in the manner that Nietzsche thought?</code></mark></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:separator -->
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity" />
<!-- /wp:separator -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In <em>Thus Spoke Zarathustra</em>, Nietzsche introduces the figure of <em>“the last man”</em> (<em>der letzte Mensch</em>). The last men are comfortable, safe, and risk-averse. They have eliminated suffering, ambition, danger, transcendence. They have optimized life for convenience.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:quote -->
<blockquote class="wp-block-quote"><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>“We have discovered happiness,” say the last men — and they blink.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></blockquote>
<!-- /wp:quote -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The “blink” suggests complacency, dullness, a kind of spiritual cataract. They no longer strain toward anything higher. No longing, no self-overcoming, no danger, no creative tension. They prefer warmth, health, modest pleasures, and social peace. They mock those who still desire greatness.  </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Nietzsche contrasts the last men with the "Overman" (<em>Übermensch</em>) — not a biological superior being, but an image of the rare individual who masters self-creation and value-generation.  </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><mark style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)" class="has-inline-color has-vivid-cyan-blue-color"><code>Is contemporary technocratic culture, where do you see the spirit of <em>der letzte Mensch</em> or <em>Übermensch</em>?</code></mark></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:separator -->
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity" />
<!-- /wp:separator -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If Max Weber was right that science cannot answer questions of meaning, and secular society's multitude of value spheres are often in conflict, how will consensus regarding values - even scientific values - be attained?</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>One possible answer to this question is that there are values - if not inherent in, then at least presupposed by - scientific rationality. The idea being that - <em>if</em> we can agree upon truth as a value, and a methodology for verification - <em>then</em> the resulting science might provide a body of facts that we can agree upon prior to beginning the difficult (democratic) process of negotiating values. Maybe.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I think both Max Weber and Friedrich Nietzsche would agree that there are values presupposed by science, but (as I read them) neither would expect all people (even all scientists) to chose `truth' as their supreme value.  </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>As for me - the idea that scientific rationality is going to somehow come to our rescue and resolve the issue of competing values in a secular and/or religiously pluralistic age - well, this strikes as overly optimistic, to say the least (and perhaps a bit <a rel="noreferrer noopener" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientism" target="_blank">scientistic</a>, a term that we will discuss down the road).  </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Why do I say `overly optimistic'?  </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Consider how the journalist Ron Suskind describes the attitude of a White House official [widely speculated to be Karl Rove] in the&nbsp;George W. Bush administration,</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:quote -->
<blockquote class="wp-block-quote"><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The aide said that guys like me were 'in what we call the reality-based community,' which he defined as people who 'believe that solutions emerge from your judicious study of discernible reality.' […] 'That's not the way the world really works anymore,' he continued. 'We're an empire now, and when we act, we create our own reality. And while you're studying that reality—judiciously, as you will—we'll act again, creating other new realities, which you can study too, and that's how things will sort out. We're history's actors…and you, all of you, will be left to just study what we do'. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></blockquote>
<!-- /wp:quote -->
