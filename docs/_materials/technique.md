<p>Technology is <em>the application of scientific knowledge for practical purposes</em>, or <em>machinery and equipment developed from the application of scientific knowledge</em> (Oxford Dictionary).  Technique is <em>a way of carrying out a particular task</em>, or <em>a skillful or efficient way of doing or achieving something</em>.   </p>


<p>As in "Propaganda is a scientific <em><strong>technique</strong> </em>that is used to influence public opinion." </p>


<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>In our technological society, technique is the totality of methods rationally arrived at and having absolute efficiency (for a given stage of development) in every field of human activity.</p>
<cite> Jacques Ellul</cite></blockquote>


<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity" />


<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<p>Ellul distills the essential characteristics of technique to a list of seven. The two most obvious ones, he says, have been addressed so often by other scholars that he can set them aside: <em>rationality</em> (for example, systematization and standardization) and <em>artificiality</em> (subjugation and often the destruction of nature). The other five characteristics of technique are less widely discussed. They are <em>automatism</em>, which is the process of technical means asserting themselves according to mathematical standards of efficiency; <em>self-augmentation</em>, the process of technical advances multiplying at a growing rate and building on each other, while the number of technicians also increases; <em>wholeness</em>, the feature of all individual techniques and their various uses sharing a common essence; <em>universalism</em>, the fact that technique and technicians are spreading worldwide; and <em>autonomy</em>, the phenomenon of technique as a closed system, “a reality in itself … with its special laws and its own determinations.”</p>
