<!-- wp:quote -->
<blockquote class="wp-block-quote"><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Neoliberalism makes citizens into consumers. The freedom of the citizen yields to the passivity of the consumer.&nbsp; As consumers, today's voters have no real interest in politics - in actively shaping the community.&nbsp; They possess neither the will nor the ability to participate in communal, political action.&nbsp; They react only passively to politics: grumbling and complaining, as consumers do about a commodity or service they do not like. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Of our own free will, we put any and all conceivable information about ourselves on the internet, without having the slightest idea who knows what, when or on what occasion.&nbsp; This lack of control represents a crisis of freedom to be taken seriously.&nbsp; Indeed, given the data that people make available willy-nilly, the very idea of protecting privacy (Datenshuntz) is becoming obsolete. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --><cite>Byung-Chul Han, Psychopolitics: Neoliberalism and New Technologies of Power</cite></blockquote>
