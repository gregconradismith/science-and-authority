<div class="wp-block-media-text alignwide is-stacked-on-mobile wp-embed-aspect-16-9 wp-has-aspect-ratio" style="grid-template-columns:38% auto;"><figure class="wp-block-media-text__media"><img src="../../wp-content/uploads/2022/09/product_650.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-3979 size-full" /></figure><div class="wp-block-media-text__content">
<p>Based on the play by Bertolt Brecht (originally translated by Charles Laughton), <em>Galileo</em> explores not merely the infamous historical figure, but the philosophical concepts for which he was both celebrated and condemned.   "Written and produced in the shadow of the anti-Communist witch hunt that ultimately caused both Brecht and Losey's permanent exit from the United States, this story of the seventeenth-century Italian astronomer – forced by the Catholic Church to recant the scientific discoveries that ran counter to religious doxa – had an obvious timeliness. But in typically Brechtian (and Loseyesque) fashion, this Galileo is no hero. Oscillating between narcissism and cowardice, <em>Galileo</em> instead illustrates the necessity, and difficulty, of ethics...." [<a rel="noreferrer noopener" href="https://harvardfilmarchive.org/calendar/galileo-2008-07" target="_blank">Harvard Film Archive</a>]</p>
