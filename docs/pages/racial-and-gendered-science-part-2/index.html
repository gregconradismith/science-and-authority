Naturalized epistemology (a term coined by W. V. O. Quine) is a collection of philosophic views concerned with the theory of knowledge that emphasize the role of natural scientific methods. This shifts focus to the empirical processes of knowledge acquisition and away from many traditional philosophical questions.

Adapted from Wikipedia

Social epistemology refers to a broad set of approaches that can be taken in epistemology (the study of knowledge) that construes human knowledge as a collective achievement. Another way of characterizing social epistemology is as the evaluation of the social dimensions of knowledge or information.

Wikipedia

Feminist epistemology and philosophy of science studies the ways in which gender does and ought to influence our conceptions of knowledge, knowers, and practices of inquiry and justification. It identifies how dominant conceptions and practices of knowledge attribution, acquisition, and justification disadvantage women and other subordinated groups, and strives to reform them to serve the interests of these groups. 

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy

Feminisms

Feminism means different things to different people. Some historians distinguish Four Waves of American Feminism:

  • First Wave: 1848 - 1920
  • Second Wave: 1963 - 1980s
  • ​​Third Wave: 1990s -
  • Fourth Wave: Present Day

While critics of the “wave” concept argue that it oversimplifies a more complicated history, it is helpful to compare and contrast the specific concerns of feminism in the above time frames.
