Professional Authority

Overview

Sociologists have introduced the term rationalization for the process of regularization and standardization in the historical development of professions. In Reed's analysis of professional authority of doctors and lawyers, rationalization is juxtaposed to an ecological model of how professions compete and change over time.1

Assignment

  • Listen to What Is Authority? – Isaac Ariail Reed – Lecture 2 – Professional Authority. [40 min]
  • Watch The Experimenter - a film directed by Michael Almereyda (Magnolia Pictures, 2015) about the American social psychologist Stanley Milgram, best known for his controversial experiments on obedience at Yale in the 1960s. Available to W&M students through SWEM Kanopy. [1 hr 50 min]

Prepare to Discuss

Reed uses Doctors and Lawyers as example authorites that arise in part from "professional autonomy."

In preparation for in-class discussion, think about how Scientists compare to Doctors and Lawyers in regard to professional autonomy.

There is a scene in the The Experimenter where an elephant follows Milgram down the hallway while he delivers an aside to the audience about the importance of the Holocaust to his work. "As Milgram notes here, the experiment wrapped up in 1962, just days before the execution of Adolf Eichmann, the Nazi official whose capture by Mossad and televised trial in Israel helped snap the West’s myopia about the Holocaust. It also allowed Hannah Arendt, describing Eichmann’s `I was just doing my job' bureaucratic blandness, to introduce `the banality of evil' into the world’s moral conversation." (Chris Barsanti, The Banality of Evil in the Experimenter, Popmatters)

The `elephant in the room' may represent the irony or problematics of Milgram's work.
