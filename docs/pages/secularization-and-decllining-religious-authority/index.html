Modernization has involved secularization of institutions, but not necessarily a decline in religion

A longstanding consensus around classical versions of secularization theory has broken down in recent decades. Religion's stubborn refusal to disappear has prompted a major reevaluation of inherited models of secularization. ... The “facts” are not much disputed: New religious movements continue to arise, older movements like Pentecostalism and Mormonism are expanding; religious fundamentalisms thrive throughout the world; and, at least in the U.S., substantial segments of the population continue to participate in orthodox organized religion. (Chaves 1994)
