Assignment: Email me a description of your final project

Please prepare a short description of your proposed final project and email it to me (gdsmit@wm.edu) before class on Tuesday.

Your description should briefly explain the subject you want to investigate and why it matters to you in the context of this course. It should identify the main question, problem, or theme you plan to explore and make clear what aspect of the topic will be your focus.

Keep the proposal specific enough that it can realistically be developed into a strong final project. If your topic is broad, narrow it to a manageable angle, case, controversy, institution, text, or example.

Your project description should also indicate the boundaries of your plan. In other words, explain what you intend to examine and, if useful, what you do not intend to argue or cover. This helps clarify the direction and scope of the work.

Finally, state the form your final project will take. For example, you might propose an essay, podcast, video, presentation, or another format approved for the course.

Aim for one well-developed paragraph, or two short paragraphs if needed. The goal is not to produce a polished final argument yet, but to show that you have a clear, focused, and workable idea.

Example

Science & Authority
Meaghan Jain
Proposed Final Project

My inspiration comes from Classes 14, 15, and 16 on overmedicalization and selling sickness. I am particularly interested in women's healthcare, and those discussions sparked my interest in exploring the prescription of hormonal contraceptives for purposes beyond pregnancy prevention (for symptoms such as cycle irregularities, period discomfort, acne, etc.). I am planning to focus on oral hormonal contraception specifically, rather than hormonal contraception broadly, to keep the scope manageable for research.

The central question I want to explore is: What does the routine, widespread prescription of oral hormonal contraceptives as a first-line response reveal about women's healthcare? I would like to examine this through a few potential subtopics: institutional convenience (individualized care vs. routine practice), differences in research on the female reproductive system compared to male counterparts, and the effects of these on patient trust in medical authority during provider interactions.

The project would not be an argument about whether oral hormonal contraception is good or bad. It would be an examination of the system that shapes women's healthcare decisions and what this means for individualized care.

The final deliverable will be a podcast.
